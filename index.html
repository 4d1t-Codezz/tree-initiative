TREE: Together for Restoration and Environmental Equality

Planting trees in Houston’s hottest neighborhoods

A student-run nonprofit started by Vedika Tandon and run by Vedika, Neha Ravi and Juliette Oudinot. We plant and care for trees in the parts of Houston that have the least shade.

TREE at a glance

The mission

Some Houston neighborhoods are up to 17° hotter than others because they have so few trees. TREE plants trees there, comes back to keep them alive, and teaches residents how to look after them.

Our goals →

The impact

More than 1,000 trees planted along the Columbia Tap Trail and in Alief, working with 8 organizations and 300+ volunteers, and recognized by National Geographic.

See the numbers →

Why we plant trees

Many of Houston’s lower-income neighborhoods have very few trees, which makes them urban heat islands. Residents there deal with more heat, worse air and more flooding, and that affects their physical and mental health.

We want Houston to be greener, healthier and fairer for everyone who lives here.

Our goals

Everything TREE does comes back to three goals.

  1. 1

    Raise awareness

    Help Houstonians see how many of their neighbors are affected by environmental injustice, and what they can do to help.

  2. 2

    Plant trees

    Plant thousands of trees in the neighborhoods with the highest summer temperatures and the least tree cover.

  3. 3

    Care for trees

    New trees are fragile for their first few months, so TREE goes back to water and mulch them until they can survive on their own.

The team

TREE was started by Vedika Tandon and is run by three students.

Vedika Tandon

Founder & President

Vedika started TREE after reading research showing that Houston’s lower-income neighborhoods have far fewer trees, and are much hotter, than wealthier ones. The research came from Rice University’s Kinder Institute, HARC and environmental-justice scholar Dr. Robert Bullard.

  • Set up TREE’s partnerships with 8 organizations, including Trees for Houston and Friends of Columbia Tap
  • Organized five plantings and a tree-care day in Third Ward and Alief
  • Planned and hosted a public workshop with speakers from Friends of Columbia Tap and Trees for Houston

Background research

These sources are the basis for the TREE Initiative. They document environmental inequality in Houston and around the world.

Rice University Kinder Institute for Urban Research

Houston’s poorer areas are left unshaded

Using the Tree Equity Score, the Kinder Institute found that socioeconomically disadvantaged areas and communities of color suffer the most from urban tree inequality in the U.S. It also found a gap of about 14% in tree canopy between the wealthiest and the poorest neighborhoods.

Read the Urban Edge article ↗

The Guardian

How America’s treeless streets are fueling inequality

“You can’t even be on the street because of the heat,” said Adelea-Lopez, who takes the bus to her seamstress job. “There aren’t a lot of trees. There are a lot of apartments. A lot of cement.”
Read the article ↗

HARC

A Case for Cool Trees

“Trees infuse resilience into urban communities by mitigating extreme heat, alleviating flood risk, capturing carbon, conserving energy, improving environmental quality, and contributing to community health and wellbeing. However, trees are not evenly distributed across Houston, which means all residents do not experience the benefits they provide.”
Read the report ↗

Harris County

Climate Justice and Action Plan

In 2023, the Harris County Commissioners Court Office of Sustainability created a countywide, equity-centered Climate Justice and Action Plan. It aims to increase collaboration with communities, put community voices at the center of decisions, and show how equity should drive climate action planning.

Dr. Robert D. Bullard

The “Father of Environmental Justice”

Dr. Bullard was one of TREE’s biggest inspirations. He studied environmental inequality in depth and was one of the first advocates to push for all people to have equal protection under environmental regulations.
